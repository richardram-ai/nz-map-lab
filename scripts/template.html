Sydney Street Map Lab

A 1 km × 1 km square centred on 16 Sydney Street, Papaioea, Palmerston North (−40.34976, 175.61954). Every layer here is free public data clipped to the square, including 2024 aerial photos and 2018 LiDAR from LINZ, plus a drawing tool for adding your own notes on top. Click anything on the map to see what the data says about it.

    Knowledge gaps to research

    • Licences that block a paid offerMost iNaturalist records are CC BY‑NC (non‑commercial). Showing them inside a paid service needs checking, or should be limited to linking out. Several Horizons layers state no licence in their metadata at all.
    • Your LINZ key can view but not exportThe key loads aerial tiles from LINZ Data Service (data.linz.govt.nz), but exports need the “exports:write” scope. The LiDAR here came from LINZ's open elevation store on AWS, which needs no key. Census boundaries from Stats NZ (datafinder.stats.govt.nz) still need an account.
    • The newest LiDAR skips the cityThe 2024 regional survey does not cover central Palmerston North, so this map uses the 2018 city survey. The canopy layer is six years older than the aerial photo. Trees planted or removed since 2018 will be wrong.
    • Checking the canopy figureAbout CANOPY% canopy over 3 m is a first estimate. Buildings missing from OpenStreetMap would count as trees, and power lines can too. Compare it with a council or university urban canopy study before quoting it.
    • Reading the flood modelThe Horizons 200‑year layer for Palmerston North is a “rapid model” of stormwater runoff, and its depth classes are not explained in the data. Ask Horizons or PNCC how it should be shown to the public.
    • National layers are too coarse for streetsLand cover, soils and liquefaction each return one or two shapes for the whole square. They suit catchment‑scale groups, not a street‑scale project. Test them again at 5–10 km.
    • Where the data lives is scatteredThe council and regional data came from their ArcGIS Online services, not from data.govt.nz, whose catalogue blocks automated access. A list of each council's service address will be a reusable asset for the offer.
    • Hosting the base mapMap tiles from other sites do not load inside a Claude preview, so this page draws its own base from OpenStreetMap data. On your own Cloudflare Pages site the OpenFreeMap and LINZ options will work. That build is the next test.
    • Sharing annotations between peopleAnnotations here are saved in your browser only. An organisation will want a shared store, a Google Sheet or a small database, with a decision about who can edit.
    • Location privacyiNaturalist hides the exact location of sensitive species and of observers who ask for it. Any annotation layer that records nests, threatened plants or volunteers' homes needs the same option.
